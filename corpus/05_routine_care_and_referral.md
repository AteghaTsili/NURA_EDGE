# Routine care, check-ins, and referral logic

## Antenatal care contact

Early and regular antenatal care saves lives. A woman should ideally begin
antenatal care in the first trimester and attend the recommended number of
contacts. Counselling on danger signs at each contact improves a woman's ability
to recognise complications early and seek care in time. Up to half of maternal
deaths can be prevented through quality antenatal care that detects and manages
complications early.

## What routine check-ins cover

Between visits, a check-in should ask about any danger signs: bleeding, severe
headache, blurred vision, swelling, fever, severe abdominal pain, reduced fetal
movement, and how the woman is feeling emotionally. Blood pressure and, where
possible, urine testing help detect pre-eclampsia. The purpose of a check-in is
to catch warning signs in the window between facility visits, when women are
usually out of contact with the health system.

## Referral logic for the assistant

The assistant grades concern by the signs present:

Refer urgently and immediately for: heavy vaginal bleeding, convulsions, severe
headache with blurred vision, severe abdominal pain with dizziness or fainting,
fever with the woman too weak to rise, fast or difficult breathing, or any
thoughts of self-harm.

Advise a facility visit soon for: sudden swelling of face or hands, reduced fetal
movement, persistent vomiting, milder abdominal pain, or feeling generally unwell.

Provide reassurance and continue routine follow-up when no danger sign is present,
while reminding the woman which signs should prompt her to seek care.

## The guiding rule

When any danger sign is present, refer rather than reassure. A false alarm is
always preferred to a missed danger sign. The assistant should be cautious,
clear, and calm, and should always ground its advice in this guidance rather than
inventing medical facts.
