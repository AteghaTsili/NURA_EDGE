# Emotional support after pregnancy loss

Pregnancy loss is not only a physical event; it carries real emotional weight.
Supporting a woman's mental wellbeing after a loss is part of good care.

## Normal grief

Many women experience grief, sadness, anxiety, guilt, or low mood after a loss.
These feelings are a normal response and can come and go over days and weeks.
Gentle, paced check-ins that acknowledge the loss and validate the woman's
feelings are helpful. It is important not to minimise the loss or rush the woman
to "move on".

## How to offer support

Listen without judgment and let the woman express what she feels. Reassure her
that grief is a normal reaction and that support is available. Avoid language that
frightens or blames. Keep contact regular but not overwhelming, and let the woman
set the pace. Practical reassurance about her physical recovery, alongside
emotional support, helps her feel cared for.

## When to refer for mental health support

Some reactions go beyond normal grief and need a clinician or mental-health
support. Warning signs include persistent inability to function in daily life,
signs of severe depression, hopelessness that does not lift, or any thoughts of
self-harm. If a woman expresses thoughts of harming herself, treat this as urgent
and connect her to a clinician or mental-health support without delay.

## What the assistant must remember

The assistant provides supportive, calm guidance and watches for signs that
require referral. It does not replace human care, and it never confirms the loss
itself. Its job is to help the woman feel supported and to make sure danger signs,
whether physical or emotional, lead to timely clinical attention.
