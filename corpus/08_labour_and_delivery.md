# Danger signs during labour and delivery

Labour and delivery carry serious risks, and in settings where facilities are far
away, recognising danger signs early and arranging urgent referral saves lives.
Across the region, the leading dangers during labour and childbirth are severe
bleeding, prolonged or obstructed labour, convulsions, and retained placenta.

## Prolonged and obstructed labour

Labour that goes on too long is dangerous for both mother and baby. Prolonged
labour is generally labour lasting more than about twelve hours, or a first and
second stage together lasting a very long time. Obstructed labour — where the baby
cannot pass through the birth canal despite strong contractions — is a
life-threatening emergency that can lead to rupture of the uterus, severe
bleeding, infection, obstetric fistula, and death of the baby.

Warning signs that labour is prolonged or obstructed include strong contractions
continuing for many hours without the baby being born, the waters having broken a
long time before with no progress, extreme exhaustion, and severe continuous
abdominal pain. Any woman with these signs needs urgent referral to a facility
that can perform a caesarean section.

## Other danger signs in labour

Severe vaginal bleeding, convulsions or fits, a hand or the cord coming out before
the baby, and the waters breaking with a bad smell (a sign of infection) are all
emergencies requiring immediate referral.

## After the baby is born

If the placenta has not come out within about thirty minutes of the birth
(retained placenta), or if there is heavy bleeding after delivery, this is an
emergency. Refer urgently.

## What the health worker should do

Treat prolonged labour, obstructed labour, severe bleeding, convulsions, a
prolapsed cord, and retained placenta as emergencies that require immediate
referral to a facility with emergency obstetric care. Because delay is the main
danger, arrange transport and referral without waiting to see if things improve.
