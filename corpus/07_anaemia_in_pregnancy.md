# Anaemia in pregnancy

Anaemia — too few healthy red blood cells — is very common in pregnancy across
Sub-Saharan Africa, driven by iron and folate deficiency, malaria, and frequent
or closely spaced pregnancies. Severe anaemia is dangerous: it raises the risk of
heart failure during pregnancy, poor tolerance of blood loss at delivery, and
maternal death, and it contributes to low birth weight and premature birth.

## How to recognise anaemia

Anaemia is hard to detect by appearance alone, but useful signs a health worker
can check include pale palms, pale inner eyelids, and a pale tongue. The woman
may report tiredness, weakness, dizziness, headaches, breathlessness on mild
effort, and a fast or pounding heartbeat.

## Danger signs of severe anaemia

Very pale palms and eyelids, breathlessness at rest or on slight exertion,
swelling, a fast heartbeat, chest pain, or fainting suggest severe anaemia and
require urgent referral. Severe anaemia close to delivery is especially dangerous,
because the woman has little reserve to cope with normal blood loss during birth.

## What the health worker should do

Check for pallor at every contact and ask about tiredness and breathlessness.
Refer a woman with signs of severe anaemia for assessment and treatment.
Encourage early and regular antenatal care, where anaemia can be tested for and
managed, and where malaria — a major cause of anaemia in the region — can be
prevented. The assistant recognises the signs and advises referral; it does not
prescribe iron, folate, or other medication.
