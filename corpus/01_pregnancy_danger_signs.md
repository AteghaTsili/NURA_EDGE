# Danger signs during pregnancy

These are the warning signs a pregnant woman or a community health worker should
recognise during pregnancy. Any one of them means the woman should be referred
to a health facility. The most serious signs require urgent, immediate referral.

## Signs requiring urgent, immediate referral

Vaginal bleeding during pregnancy is a danger sign at any stage and must be
assessed urgently. Bleeding may signal miscarriage, ectopic pregnancy, or, later
in pregnancy, a problem with the placenta.

Convulsions or fits are an emergency sign. A pregnant woman who has a convulsion
may have eclampsia, a life-threatening complication, and needs immediate referral.

Severe headache with blurred vision is a warning sign of pre-eclampsia,
especially after 20 weeks of pregnancy. Combined with high blood pressure it can
progress to eclampsia and must be assessed without delay.

Fever with the woman too weak to get out of bed suggests a serious infection and
requires urgent care.

Severe abdominal pain is a danger sign. In early pregnancy, severe abdominal pain
with dizziness or fainting may indicate an ectopic pregnancy, which is a
life-threatening emergency.

Fast or difficult breathing can indicate a serious underlying problem and needs
prompt assessment.

## Signs that mean the woman should go to a health centre soon

Swelling of the fingers, face, or legs, particularly when it appears suddenly,
can be a sign of pre-eclampsia and should be checked, especially if accompanied
by headache or visual changes.

Reduced or absent fetal movement in later pregnancy should be assessed, as it can
indicate the baby is in distress.

Milder abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, or feeling generally unwell should
prompt a visit for assessment.

## Key principle for triage

Pre-eclampsia is characterised by new high blood pressure after 20 weeks of
pregnancy. Screening involves measuring blood pressure, checking urine for
protein, and asking about danger signs such as severe headache, visual
disturbance, and swelling. Because complications can develop quickly, a woman
with any danger sign should be referred rather than reassured and sent home.
