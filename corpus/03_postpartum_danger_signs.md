# Danger signs after childbirth and after a loss

The days and weeks after childbirth or after a pregnancy loss carry real risk.
Many serious complications appear when the woman is already back home, so
recognising these signs early and referring quickly saves lives. The leading
causes of maternal death in this period are heavy bleeding, infection, and high
blood pressure.

## Postpartum haemorrhage (heavy bleeding)

Postpartum haemorrhage is severe bleeding after childbirth and is a leading cause
of maternal death. It usually happens within hours of delivery but can occur up
to twelve weeks afterwards. Warning signs include bleeding that is heavier than a
normal period or getting worse over time, passing large clots, and signs of blood
loss such as dizziness, feeling faint, or a fast heartbeat. Heavy bleeding after
delivery is an emergency and requires urgent referral. Response to heavy bleeding
is time-sensitive, so delay is dangerous.

## Infection (maternal sepsis)

Infection after childbirth or after a loss can develop into sepsis, a
life-threatening emergency. Postpartum sepsis accounts for many maternal deaths
between three and seven days after birth, when most women are at home. Warning
signs include fever above 38 degrees Celsius, foul-smelling vaginal discharge,
lower abdominal pain or tenderness, and feeling very unwell. Any of these signs
requires prompt clinical assessment and antibiotics.

## High blood pressure after delivery

Pre-eclampsia and eclampsia can occur after delivery, not only during pregnancy.
A significant share of eclamptic seizures happen after birth, sometimes days or
weeks later. Severe headache, blurred vision, upper abdominal pain, or a
convulsion in the weeks after delivery are danger signs requiring urgent referral.

## Blood clots

Pain, swelling, redness, or warmth in the leg, especially the calf, can indicate
a blood clot and should be assessed promptly.

## Summary for the health worker

In the postpartum period, treat heavy bleeding, fever with foul-smelling
discharge, severe headache or convulsion, and severe abdominal pain as danger
signs that warrant urgent referral. When in doubt, refer: a false alarm is safer
than a missed complication.
